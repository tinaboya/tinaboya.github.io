### About me

I work on trustworthy machine learning for high-stakes decision making, with healthcare as my main application area. I defended my PhD in Life Sciences (AI for Healthcare) at the [Université de Genève](https://www.unige.ch/medecine/radio/en/research-groups/1035teodoro) in September 2026, in the [Data Science for Digital Health](https://ds4dh.unige.ch/) group of Prof. Douglas Teodoro. From November 2025 to April 2026 I was a visiting researcher at MIT's [Laboratory for Computational Physiology](https://lcp.mit.edu/) with Prof. Leo A. Celi.

Before my PhD, I obtained an MSc in Electrical Engineering (ir., with Distinction) from Vrije Universiteit Brussel / Université libre de Bruxelles in 2021 and a BEng in Information Engineering from [Northwestern Polytechnical University](https://en.nwpu.edu.cn/) in 2019, and I was a research intern at the [Center for Law & Economics, ETH Zurich](https://lawecon.ethz.ch/) in 2021.

### Research interests

Trustworthy machine learning for high-stakes decision making:

- **Transparent human–AI interaction**: reliable evidence-based decision support systems.
- **Robustness**: failure-mode analysis of models deployed in real-world settings.
- **Fairness**: how model behaviour differs across languages and cultures.

### Research highlights

- **Retrieval**: a ranking framework based on discriminative models that automatically detects usefulness, supportiveness and credibility signals across a billion web pages, improving the quality of health search results for lay users ([JMIR AI 2024](https://doi.org/10.2196/42630)).
- **Verification**: the CliniFact benchmark for clinical claim verification, where small discriminative models outperformed large generative models at clinical natural language inference ([Scientific Data 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-025-04417-x)).
- **Generation**: the HealthContradict benchmark for biomedical retrieval-augmented generation, showing that in-domain fine-tuning helps models identify the correct context ([npj Digital Medicine 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-025-02336-0)).
- **PAINT**: led a 17-author study across 16 institutions on disparities in how pain is described in medical text across four languages ([MRL @ EMNLP 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.mrl-1.51/)).
